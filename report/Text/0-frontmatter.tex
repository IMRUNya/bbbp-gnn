\NameOfAbstract{Abstract}
\begin{abstract}

Blood--brain barrier penetration was predicted for the 2039 molecules of the MoleculeNet BBBP dataset, comparing a learned graph representation against a fixed substructure fingerprint under two splitting protocols. An edge-aware graph isomorphism network (three \texttt{GINEConv} layers, 67\,588 parameters) and a logistic regression on 2048-bit ECFP4 fingerprints were trained on 1965 deduplicated molecules split 70:15:15 at random and by Bemis--Murcko scaffold, with three seeds each. On the scaffold split the network reached a test ROC-AUC of $0.885 \pm 0.025$ against the baseline's $0.876$; on the random split, $0.912 \pm 0.008$ against $0.892$. The baseline obtained higher accuracy and $F_1$ on both splits because the network ranks well but calibrates poorly. The split strategy shifted validation ROC-AUC by $0.0354$, about seven times the largest seed spread, and the random split's subsets shared 238 scaffolds against zero for the scaffold split. The scaffold-split difference between the two models is smaller than the seed standard deviation, so they are statistically indistinguishable there.

\end{abstract}
